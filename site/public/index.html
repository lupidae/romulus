Postgres, down to the last policy.

romulus keeps what the Postgres catalog says, with remus's model. Drafts compile in PostgreSQL 17, so the graph shows what Postgres accepted.

  • ENUMEnums, with their values in order
  • DOMAINDomains, with their base type and checks
  • PARTITION BYPartitioned tables, partitions kept under their parent
  • CREATE POLICYRow level security policies and their roles
  • GENERATEDIdentity and generated columns
  • CHECKCheck, unique and exclusion constraints
  • WHEREPartial and expression indexes
  • COMMENT ONComments on tables and columns, shown on the graph
  • CREATE VIEWViews, with the tables they read
  • citext[]Arrays and composite types

Roles, functions and extensions your SQL uses without creating are stubbed for the compile, and never reach your model.

Questions

Something else? Write to us.

What does “DBA as a service” mean here?

romulus does the schema work a DBA would keep for a team: it keeps every version, shows exactly what changed, checks each proposal in real Postgres before anyone publishes it, and gives everyone one place to design and review the next change.

Does romulus connect to our production database?

No. You upload SQL, or remus reads your database inside your own CI and pushes the schema. romulus never holds a connection string and never sees your data.

Does it replace our migrations?

No. Keep your migration tool; romulus versions the schema it produces. Push the result from CI after each run, and use drafts to agree on what the next migration should do.

Which is the source of truth, the SQL or the graph?

The SQL. Edits on the graph are written back as SQL statements, so the text is always what gets compiled and versioned. Table positions and groups are saved next to it.

Which Postgres versions does it understand?

Drafts compile in a PostgreSQL 17 sandbox, and pushes from remus carry whatever your own server's catalog says. Enums, domains, partitions, policies and generated columns all come through as themselves.

How do people sign in?

With single sign-on. An admin invites teammates with a one-time link, which only works for the verified email address it was made for.

What does it cost?

Nothing during early access, since there is no billing yet. Request access and we'll set up your organization.
