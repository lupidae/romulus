Comparison

romulus vs Atlas

Atlas is a schema-as-code command line: you describe the schema in HCL, SQL or your ORM, and it plans, lints and applies migrations to your databases, on many engines. romulus is where a Postgres team keeps, compares, designs and reviews its schema together, and tests each migration on rows before it merges; it hands you the files and never connects to your database. Pick Atlas to automate migrations across engines and environments, romulus to review schema changes as a team.

Compared on October 24, 2026, from Atlas's own pagesSources

In short

  • Atlas automates; romulus is a workspace. Atlas lives in your terminal and your pipelines and applies changes itself. romulus is a web app (hosted or on your servers) with an MCP server and a small CLI for CI; it writes migration files and leaves deploying to you.
  • Atlas covers more ground. More engines, ORMs as schema sources, a Terraform provider, a Kubernetes operator, drift detection and over 50 lint analyzers.
  • romulus is built around people reviewing a change. Live drafts in SQL and on a table graph, threads on the column in question, approvals, and every migration run up and down on generated rows, without writing a test.

Where Atlas is the better choice

  • Your schemas span several engines, or live in an ORM you want to stay the source of truth.
  • You want the tool to apply changes itself, from Terraform, a Kubernetes operator or a pipeline, declaratively or from versioned migrations.
  • You want a broad lint suite, drift detection on production, and tests you write for functions, triggers and data migrations.
  • Your team works in files and pull requests and wants nothing else to open.

Where romulus is the better choice

  • You are on Postgres and want the schema as something the whole team reads, comments and designs together, not only files in a repository.
  • You want each migration tried on rows before merge without writing tests: a NOT NULL on a column with nulls fails there, not in production (an example with Prisma).
  • You don't want a tool holding credentials to your databases. romulus reads SQL text, never a connection.
  • You want the whole product open source, self-hosted for free, or a flat price whatever the team's size.

A migration in romulus

The migration romulus wrote for a change, captured from a local install: each statement marked with what it can do to existing rows, ready to download for your tool.

Using both

Atlas reads a migration directory as a state, and romulus writes migrations as plain SQL files. A team can design and review a change in romulus, commit its migration, and keep Atlas for linting and applying it.
